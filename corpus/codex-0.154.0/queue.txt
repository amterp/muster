What `codex queue` does to a Codex 0.154.0 session running in a terminal.

Recorded 2026-10-03 on macOS 26.4.1 / arm64, codex-cli 0.154.0 running gpt-5.6-luna at low
reasoning effort, -a on-request -s read-only, in a 100x30 pseudo-terminal driven by a scratch
script built on `tools/detection-capture.py`.

The session took one turn, idle at its composer, and was renamed "queue-probe-<time>" with
/rename. From another process, with no CODEX_ or MUSTER_ variables:
    $ codex queue --thread queue-probe-<time> --message "Say the word GRAPE."
    Queued message <message id> for thread <session id>.    (exit 0)
The session, with nothing typed into its terminal:
    › Say the word GRAPE.
    • GRAPE
A message queued by the session's name started a turn in the idle session, as if typed there.

Recorded 2026-10-04, the same way, with a scratch SessionStart and UserPromptSubmit hook writing
its input to a file, in one session. Each `codex queue` was run from another process:

By the session id a hook was handed (session_id), not its name:
    $ codex queue --thread <session id> --message "Say the word GRAPE."
    Queued message <message id> for thread <session id>.    (exit 0)
    UserPromptSubmit "Say the word GRAPE.", and the turn answered GRAPE

At an idle composer holding "half typed", nobody's Return pressed:
    queued "Say the word MELON." (exit 0)
    › Say the word MELON.   • MELON
    the composer held "half typed" again once the turn ended

While Codex ran "sleep 15" for a turn of its own:
    queued "Say the word LEMON." (exit 0); nothing changed on screen while the turn ran
    once it ended: UserPromptSubmit "Say the word LEMON.", a turn of its own

At its approval prompt for "echo hi > made.txt":
    queued "Say the word PEACH." (exit 0)
    six seconds later the approval prompt was still up and made.txt did not exist
    Esc; the turn ended; "Say the word PEACH." was never submitted

An unknown thread:
    $ codex queue --thread no-such-thread-xyz --message ...
    Error: No active session found matching 'no-such-thread-xyz'.    (exit 1)

The same session id once that Codex had exited:
    Queued message <message id> for thread <session id>.    (exit 0)
